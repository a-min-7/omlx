Your scoping is right about decision models, and I would narrow one word. `/v1/systemone` and
`/v1/decisions` take the decision engine only when the named model is a decision model; every other
model goes through the ordinary chat path (`_chat_model_decisions` → `engine.chat`), and that is the
scheduler the accelerator attaches to. Nothing in `#5306` touches that path, so step 1 is unaffected —
that branch of those two endpoints is not.

And `#5449` has closed the hazard I was pointing at, by construction: `_LORA_CONFLICTS` makes an
adapter and `prefill_helper_node_id` mutually exclusive (with DFlash, VLM MTP, ANE prefill, MoE
offload and external engines), and a merge that carries both keeps the adapter and clears the
accelerator. Your own oQh paragraph in `#5306` names the same mechanism — fused projections skip the
adapter's wrappers — so this is not news to you. I withdraw the warning as an open item.

**Q2.** The shipped answer is better than the one I would have proposed. Selection is per loaded model
(`lora_adapter` in Model Settings, exposed to clients as `<model>:<profile>`), there is no per-request
field, and because `lora_adapter` is part of the engine's runtime signature and of the cache's
numerics revision, switching arms is a reload with a cold prefix cache rather than a second copy of
the weights. My objection to `<model>:<profile>` was memory, and on that point I was wrong.

**Then step 2, where the question is narrower than the one I was going to ask.** `#5306`'s structure
settles most of it: the decision model builds its own backbone and applies the adapter to its text
model, so "sharing the backbone" can only mean borrowing the chat model's already-loaded text model.
What it borrows is the part that matters:

> a second module tree built over the same weight arrays, or the chat model's own module objects?

The second is not safe, and silently so. `apply_lora` wraps its targets in place, and its only guard
is that a target is not *already* a `LoraLinear` — so lent module objects would be wrapped inside the
chat engine, and the plain arm becomes adapted with nothing to show for it. A chat tree that had been
loaded fused or packed fails the other way: the targets are gone, so `resolve` raises rather than
wrapping. Both are avoided by building the decision tree from the raw arrays and applying the adapter
before the fusions — the same ordering `#5449` needs — with one thing to check: the shared weights
must not be counted twice against the memory budget.

**Q3.** Under the shipped route there is nothing separate left to decide: the picker is in Model
Settings > Advanced. If step 2 adds that second tree, its control belongs in that PR.

One reconciliation while you are in here: step 1 reads the adapter with its own PEFT reader
(`models/lora.py`, on the PR head), while `#5449` merged `lora_adapters.py` — PEFT and mlx-lm, with
the discovery hook and the cache signature. If step 2 builds that second tree, both would be live at
once. Is the JEV path going to move onto `apply_adapter`?

**What I would look for on a step-2 branch.** With the accelerator enabled and an adapter-requested
prompt of ≥2,048 tokens: the plain arm's output unchanged, and the adapted request either staying
local — no helper session, the host accounting for its tokens — or the helper's status naming the
adapter it ran. My worry was only that a split with the adapter on one side must not be reachable by
accident.

The offer stands: once there is a branch, I can run it on a Qwen3.8-27B oQ4e build across a
host+helper split and report which arm served an adapter-requested long prompt, plus what switching
arms costs. I have measured that split on the dense 27B; I have not run a LoRA adapter on either
side, so nothing here is a claim about adapted output.
