Your scoping is right about decision models, and I would narrow one word. `/v1/systemone` and
`/v1/decisions` take the decision engine only when the named model is a decision model; for every
other model they go through the ordinary chat path (`_chat_model_decisions` → `engine.chat`), which
is the scheduler the accelerator attaches to. Nothing in `#5306` touches that path, so step 1 is
unaffected — the chat-model branch of those two endpoints is not, and a readout there with a long
enough input is split like any completion.

And `#5449` has closed the hazard I was pointing at, by construction: `_LORA_CONFLICTS` makes an
adapter and `prefill_helper_node_id` mutually exclusive (with DFlash, VLM MTP, ANE prefill, MoE
offload and external engines), and a merge that carries both keeps the adapter and clears the
accelerator. Your own oQh paragraph in `#5306` already names the same mechanism — fused projections
skip the adapter's wrappers — so this is not news to you. I withdraw the warning as an open item
rather than argue it.

**Q2.** The shipped answer is not the one I would have proposed, and it is better than mine on the
point I cared about. Selection is per loaded model — `lora_adapter` in Model Settings, exposed to
clients as `<model>:<profile>` — and there is no per-request field. `lora_adapter` is part of the
engine's runtime signature; switching between arms unloads and reloads, and because the adapter is
also part of the cache's numerics revision, the switch starts on a cold prefix cache for that arm.
That removes the memory objection I had to `<model>:<profile>`. The cost moves to switching — a
reload plus a cold cache — not to resident weights.

**Then step 2's design question — and I had been asking the wrong one.** I was going to ask whether
step 2 means attaching an adapter to an already-loaded model, without a reload. `#5306`'s own words
are "sharing the backbone with an already loaded chat model", which admits two different designs:

- **one engine serving both** — plain and adapted requests on the loaded chat model. That forces
  per-request selection, the accelerator hazard returns with it, and "requests that use the adapter
  stay local" is then the safe first version: no launch handshake, no adapted branch inside the
  helper, correct by construction.
- **a second decision engine over the same weight arrays** — its own module tree and KV cache,
  adapted wrappers over the chat model's already-loaded arrays, no copy and no reload. The chat
  engine keeps the accelerator, the decision engine has no helper, and the hazard does not arise.

Which one does "sharing the backbone" mean? If it is the second, three constraints belong with it:
the memory accounting must not count the arrays twice, the chat entry must not be evicted while the
decision engine holds them, and building that tree still needs the ordering right — `apply_adapter`
before the fusions and packers that replace the projections.

**Q3.** Under the shipped route there is nothing separate to decide: the picker is in Model Settings
> Advanced. If step 2 lifts the exclusion or adds a second engine, its control belongs in that PR —
a knob whose only consumer is that mechanism has nothing to review on its own.

**One reconciliation worth raising now.** Step 1 carries its own PEFT reader (`models/lora.py`,
unmerged, applied in place) while `#5449` merged `lora_adapters.py` — PEFT and mlx-lm, with the
discovery hook, the cache signature and the profile integration. Whatever step 2 becomes, those two
have to agree on reading one format. Is the intention to move the JEV path onto `apply_adapter`?

**What a step-2 branch must be able to show**, if it ever exposes both arms on one loaded model:
with the accelerator enabled and an adapter-requested prompt of ≥2,048 tokens, the request either
stayed local — no helper session for it, the host accounting for its tokens — or the helper's status
names the adapter it ran. The middle case, a split with the adapter on one side, must not be
reachable by accident.
